*The first article in Graves & Thyroid, an AskDoGood series on living with Graves’ disease and getting care for an overactive thyroid.*

I want to talk about the thyroid stuff we don’t always bring up at the doctor’s office. The extra bathroom trips. The legs that feel weak on stairs you’ve climbed a hundred times. Being worn out all day, then lying in bed wide awake.

I’ve lived with Graves’ disease, and I want AskDoGood to make room for the everyday details. Sometimes the thing you almost leave out of an appointment is worth putting at the top of your list.

Graves’ disease is an autoimmune condition that can make the thyroid produce too much hormone. That’s called **hyperthyroidism**, and it can affect your digestion, muscles, sleep, mood, and heart rhythm. Graves’ can also affect the eyes. [NIDDK’s guide to Graves’ disease](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/graves-disease)

## What it can look like in real life

- **Stairs suddenly feel harder.** New muscle weakness can make everyday tasks like climbing stairs or getting up from a chair harder.
- **You’re going to the bathroom more often.** More frequent bowel movements or diarrhea are worth mentioning, especially alongside other changes.
- **You’re exhausted and still can’t sleep.** Tell your clinician if it comes with feeling unusually hot, shaky hands, or a pounding heartbeat.
- **Your period changes.** Periods may become lighter or farther apart.
- **Your eyes feel like there’s sand in them.** Grittiness, extra tearing, puffy lids, light sensitivity, or pain behind the eyes are worth bringing up. Thyroid eye disease can occur even when thyroid hormone levels are normal.
- **The skin on your shins thickens or feels rough.** This is a rare Graves’ complication called Graves’ dermopathy. It can also affect the tops of the feet.

These symptoms can have several causes. Tell your clinician what changed, when it started, and which symptoms showed up together. [American Thyroid Association on hyperthyroidism](https://www.thyroid.org/hyperthyroidism/) · [Thyroid eye disease](https://www.thyroid.org/thyroid-eye-disease/)

## Getting through the day while you get answers

- **Make the appointment easier on yourself.** Keep a short note on your phone with what changed and how it affects your day. Bring your list of medications and supplements. Try saying, “My sleep, digestion, and strength have all changed. Could we check whether my thyroid is involved?” Your clinician may check TSH, free T4, and T3, and consider antibody testing if Graves’ is suspected.
- **Give yourself room to pace the day.** Keep a fan nearby if heat is bothering you. Break chores into smaller chunks and ask for help with heavy tasks on weak days. Ask your clinician what level of activity is appropriate if your heart has been racing or beating irregularly.
- **Bring up eye symptoms.** For mild dryness or grittiness, ask about lubricating eye drops. Contact a clinician immediately if you can’t fully close an eyelid, colors look different, or part of your vision goes missing. [American Thyroid Association guidance on thyroid eye disease](https://www.thyroid.org/thyroid-eye-disease/)

**When it’s an emergency.** A high fever with a pounding heartbeat and confusion can signal thyroid storm, a rare, life-threatening emergency. Call 911. [MedlinePlus on thyroid storm](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000400.htm)
